The Engine: Volatility & Correlation

The LMM simulates thousands of potential paths for future interest rates. The two key dials that control these simulations are volatility (how much each rate moves) and correlation (how much rates move together).

Life After LIBOR: The Transition to SOFR

The end of LIBOR in June 2023 marked a new era. New benchmarks like SOFR are fundamentally different, presenting major challenges for models designed for LIBOR.

LIBOR (Legacy)

  • ⚠️
    Credit Risk: Included bank credit risk, as it was based on unsecured lending.
  • ➡️
    Forward-Looking: Term rates were known at the start of an interest period.
  • ✍️
    Subjective: Based on panel bank estimates, which were prone to manipulation.

SOFR (New Standard)

  • 🛡️
    Nearly Risk-Free: Secured by Treasury collateral, removing bank credit risk.
  • ⬅️
    Backward-Looking: Term rates are compounded and known at the end of an interest period.
  • 📊
    Data-Driven: Based on a huge volume of actual, observable repo market transactions.

This requires a fundamental rethinking of market models, leading to the development of new "RFR-Native" frameworks.
